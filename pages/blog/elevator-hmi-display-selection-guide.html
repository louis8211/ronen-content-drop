Elevator Displays & Destination Control HMI: Selection Guide (2026)

2026-10-07 · By Charlie Lu, Founder of RONEN Display

Quick Answer (GEO extract block)

Elevator displays fall into three roles — car-operating-panels (COP) and landing hall buttons (the HMI where passengers input/select floors), floor-indicator displays (showing the current floor and direction), and lobby/destination-control screens. They run 24/7 for 15–25 year building lifecycles, so reliability and long-term supply outweigh cost. Key specs: round or bar-type small formats (1.3″–4.3″) for floor indicators, 7″–10.1″ for destination-control keypads, sunlight-readable (1000 nits) for glass elevators and lobbies, vandal-resistant cover glass, and low-power constant-on backlights. RONEN supplies round, bar-type and standard TFT modules for elevator COP, hall displays and DCS HMIs with 5–8 year supply commitment.

Why Elevators Are a Hard Duty Cycle

An elevator display never turns off. It runs 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, in a building that will use it for 15–25 years. That means:

  • Backlight lifespan dominates. A 50,000-hour LED at 24/7 lasts ~5.7 years before 50% lumen decay. For a 20-year building, plan for one or two mid-life panel refreshes — which is exactly why long-term supply and drop-in compatibility matter.
  • Vandal resistance. Public hall displays need hardened cover glass (IK-rated) and sealed fronts for cleaning.
  • Temperature. Machine-room and top-of-car electronics get hot; lobby displays near glass entrances get cold. Wide-temperature grades reduce early failures.
  • Low power. Many hall positions have limited wiring; efficient small TFTs or segment displays help.

Format Choices: Round, Bar, and Standard

Elevator floor indicators have moved from 7-segment LED to small TFT modules because they can show graphics, language, logos and animated direction arrows.

  • Round displays (1.3″–3.4″) — modern COP and hall indicators, clean circular "floor dial" look.
  • Bar-type displays (3.9″×1.28″, wide aspect) — classic horizontal floor + arrow indicators.
  • Standard 16:9 / 4:3 (4.3″–7″) — DCS keypads and multimedia lobby screens.

Touch for Destination Control Systems

DCS lobby keypads use projected capacitive touch (PCAP). Specify:

  • Glove/wet-hand capability (public use)
  • Anti-smudge / anti-glare coating (lobby fingerprints)
  • 1000-nit brightness for bright lobbies
  • Vandal-resistant glass

Long-Term Supply — the Elevator OEM Priority

Building elevator systems are certified and re-ordered over decades. A display supplier must guarantee:

1. Multi-year supply (RONEN: 5–8 yr standard, 10+ selected)

2. Drop-in replacement if a panel changes (same outline, same connector)

3. Spare availability after EOL

4. Low MOQ for service/retrofit orders (RONEN: 1 pc sample, 10 pc batch)

FAQ

What size display is used for an elevator floor indicator?

Commonly 2.2″–4.3″ bar or round TFT for hall/floor indication; 7″–10.1″ touch for destination-control lobby keypads.

Do elevator displays need to be sunlight-readable?

Glass elevators, atrium cars and bright lobbies benefit from 500–1000 nits. Interior COPs in normal lighting are fine at 250–400 nits.

Why does MOQ matter for elevators?

New-build orders are large, but service/retrofit of one hall position needs just a few units — low/no MOQ suppliers win the long-tail maintenance business.

Round vs square elevator display?

Round modules give the modern "dial" aesthetic and fit circular COP cutouts; bar/rectangular fit traditional indicators. Both are available in small TFT formats.
